# Discovery and Strategy

## Goal

Turn a vague request such as “make it premium, animated, and 3D” into a clear creative and commercial direction without trapping the user in a long questionnaire.

## Fast discovery

First inspect the repository and assets. Then fill as much of this brief as possible:

- Product name and category
- Primary product or offer
- Target customer
- Market and language
- Primary conversion action
- Price, bundle, or promotion
- Strongest benefit
- Product differentiator or mechanism
- Top three objections
- Available proof
- Available visual assets
- Brand constraints
- Desired emotional response
- Required platform or framework

Ask questions only for gaps that materially change the design or implementation.

## Good high-leverage questions

- What exact product and offer should the page sell?
- Who is the main customer and market?
- What is the single action the visitor should take?
- Which real assets are available now?
- Which references feel right, and what should be avoided?

Avoid asking the user to choose technical libraries unless they have a genuine preference. Make the technical decision after inspecting the project.

## Creative-direction format

When presenting directions, make them meaningfully different:

### Direction name

- **Concept:** one sentence describing the visual world
- **Emotional tone:** what the visitor should feel
- **Palette:** dominant, supporting, and accent behavior
- **Typography:** display and utility character
- **Composition:** grid, density, image scale, whitespace
- **Signature interaction:** one memorable motion or 3D moment
- **Product treatment:** how the pack, texture, or result is shown
- **Conversion effect:** how this direction supports trust and action
- **Risk:** performance, asset, or clarity risk

Do not present three versions that only change colors.

## Choosing a direction

Recommend the direction that best balances:

1. product truth;
2. audience expectations;
3. available assets;
4. distinctiveness;
5. mobile feasibility;
6. conversion clarity;
7. implementation cost and performance.

## Landing-page brief template

Create `docs/LANDING_PAGE_BRIEF.md` with:

```markdown
# Landing Page Brief

## Objective
## Product and offer
## Audience and market
## Primary CTA
## Product promise
## Differentiators
## Objections
## Proof and trust assets
## Message hierarchy
## Page narrative
## Visual concept
## Typography and palette
## Signature interaction
## Motion and 3D plan
## Mobile strategy
## Performance and accessibility constraints
## Existing assets
## Missing assets
## Assumptions
## Definition of done
```

## Definition of done

A strong brief answers these questions:

- What should the visitor understand in five seconds?
- Why should they believe it?
- What should they do next?
- What makes this page visually ownable by the brand?
- Which animation deserves the most implementation effort?
- What remains fully functional when motion or WebGL is unavailable?
