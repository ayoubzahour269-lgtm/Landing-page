# Conversion Architecture and Product Copy

## First viewport

The first viewport should communicate:

- what the product is;
- the strongest relevant benefit;
- who or what it is for when necessary;
- the primary action;
- at least one trust or offer cue when available.

Avoid opening with a vague brand manifesto that delays product understanding.

## Message hierarchy

Write from concrete to supporting detail:

1. Product promise
2. Differentiator or mechanism
3. Proof
4. Supporting benefits
5. Usage and fit
6. Offer
7. Objection handling

## Hero copy pattern

- **Eyebrow:** category, offer, or proof cue
- **Headline:** specific desired outcome or distinctive promise
- **Support:** one or two sentences explaining how or for whom
- **CTA:** direct action
- **Micro-proof:** delivery, rating, guarantee, bundle, or another verified cue

Do not use several competing CTAs in the hero.

## Benefit writing

Weak: “Premium formula”

Stronger: “A dense mousse that spreads quickly from roots to lengths.”

Weak: “Revolutionary results”

Stronger: “Even application in a familiar shampoo-like gesture.”

Use observable product truths. Do not invent evidence.

## Cosmetics and wellness claims

Do not write unsupported medical, therapeutic, clinical, permanent, guaranteed, or absolute claims.

Distinguish among:

- appearance claims;
- sensory claims;
- functional cosmetic claims;
- measured claims with supplied evidence;
- regulated or medical claims requiring substantiation.

When evidence is missing, use accurate cautious language and list what proof would strengthen the page.

## Proof hierarchy

Prefer, when genuinely available:

1. Demonstration or visible product mechanism
2. Measured result with source
3. Verified customer reviews
4. Expert or authority endorsement
5. Detailed ingredient or material explanation
6. Guarantees, delivery, and return reassurance

Never fabricate reviews, star ratings, customer counts, laboratory results, press logos, or certifications.

## Offer section

Make the decision easy to understand:

- exact product or bundle;
- included quantity or duration;
- price and verified comparison price;
- variant or shade selection;
- delivery information;
- verified guarantee or return terms;
- primary CTA;
- relevant trust details.

Visually prioritize the offer over decorative motion.

## FAQ

Use FAQs to resolve real objections, not repeat marketing claims. Common categories:

- product fit;
- application or setup;
- expected result;
- variant selection;
- safety or compatibility information supplied by the brand;
- shipping;
- returns;
- payment.

## Mobile conversion

- Keep CTA labels short.
- Do not put crucial proof only in hover states.
- Ensure tap targets are comfortable.
- Avoid carousels that hide all evidence behind swipes.
- Keep variant and quantity controls visible and understandable.
- Use a sticky CTA only after the visitor has enough context and only if it does not obscure content.
